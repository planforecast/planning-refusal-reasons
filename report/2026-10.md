# The Plan Forecast Planning Decisions Report: October 2026 edition

Across the 9 councils we read (Cheshire West and Chester, Ealing, Leeds, North Yorkshire, Salford, Stockport, Trafford, Wakefield, York), 30,802 householder planning applications were decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (for Ealing, since March 2025; for Wakefield, since June 2025). How many were approved, how long they took, and why the refused were refused, council by council and kind of work by kind of work. Frozen for the October 2026 edition: a figure quoted from it stays true.

## Key findings

1. Across the 9 councils we read, 89% of the 30,802 householder planning applications decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (for Ealing, since March 2025; for Wakefield, since June 2025) were approved (Plan Forecast, Planning Decisions Report, October 2026 edition).
2. Across the 9 councils we read, half of the 30,798 householder planning applications decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (for Ealing, since March 2025; for Wakefield, since June 2025) were decided within 55 days, and three quarters within 69 days (Plan Forecast, Planning Decisions Report, October 2026 edition).
3. Of the 9 councils we read, Wakefield approved the highest share of householder planning applications: 96% of the 889 it decided from June 2025 to September 2026 (Plan Forecast, Planning Decisions Report, October 2026 edition).
4. Of the 9 councils we read, Leeds approved the lowest share of householder planning applications: 83% of the 7,562 it decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (Plan Forecast, Planning Decisions Report, October 2026 edition).
5. Of the 9 councils we read, Stockport decided householder planning applications quickest: half of the 3,098 it decided in the 36 months to September 2026 were decided within 42 days (Plan Forecast, Planning Decisions Report, October 2026 edition).
6. Of the 9 councils we read, Cheshire West and Chester took longest to decide householder planning applications: half of the 3,299 it decided in the 36 months to September 2026 took 65 days or more (Plan Forecast, Planning Decisions Report, October 2026 edition).
7. Of the 4,601 refused planning applications decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (for Ealing, since March 2025; for Wakefield, since June 2025) whose decision notices we read, across 9 councils, 44% were refused at least in part over design or the character of the area (Plan Forecast, Planning Decisions Report, October 2026 edition).
8. Across the 9 councils we read, new dwellings in gardens were approved least often of the kinds of work we track: 75% of the 583 decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (for Ealing, since March 2025; for Wakefield, since June 2025) (Plan Forecast, Planning Decisions Report, October 2026 edition).
9. Of the 4,601 refused planning applications decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (for Ealing, since March 2025; for Wakefield, since June 2025) whose decision notices we read, across 9 councils, 26% were refused at least in part over the effect on neighbours (Plan Forecast, Planning Decisions Report, October 2026 edition).
10. Of the 4,601 refused planning applications decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (for Ealing, since March 2025; for Wakefield, since June 2025) whose decision notices we read, across 9 councils, 19% were refused at least in part over work outside permitted development (Plan Forecast, Planning Decisions Report, October 2026 edition).

## Questions this report answers

### What share of householder planning applications are approved?

Across the 9 councils we read (Cheshire West and Chester, Ealing, Leeds, North Yorkshire, Salford, Stockport, Trafford, Wakefield and York), 89% of the 30,802 householder planning applications decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (for Ealing, since March 2025; for Wakefield, since June 2025) were approved; the rest were refused. Withdrawn applications are left out of the rate.

### How long does planning permission take for a householder application?

Across the 9 councils we read, half of the 30,798 householder planning applications decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (for Ealing, since March 2025; for Wakefield, since June 2025) were decided within 55 days of being validated (or received, where the register gives no validation day). A quarter were decided within 47 days, and three quarters within 69 days.

### Which council approves the most householder planning applications?

Of the 9 councils we read, Wakefield approved the highest share (96% of 889) and Leeds the lowest (83% of 7,562). The report's tables rank every council with enough decisions to say.

### Which council decides householder planning applications quickest?

Of the 9 councils we read, Stockport decided householder planning applications quickest: half of the 3,098 it decided in the 36 months to September 2026 were decided within 42 days. The report's tables rank every council with enough decisions to say.

### Why is planning permission refused?

Of the 4,601 refused applications decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (for Ealing, since March 2025; for Wakefield, since June 2025) whose decision notices we read, the reasons most often given were about design or the character of the area (44%), the effect on neighbours (26%) and work outside permitted development (19%). A refusal often gives more than one reason, so the shares add up to more than 100%.

### How often is planning permission granted for rear extensions?

Across the 9 councils we read, 92% of the 10,242 rear extensions decided in the 36 months to September 2026 (for Ealing, since March 2025; for Wakefield, since June 2025) were approved, and half were decided within 53 days. Withdrawn applications are left out of the rate.

### Where do these figures come from?

From each council's own planning register, read by Plan Forecast: every decision the register lists in the window that we sorted into one of the 9 kinds of work we track, counted by council and kind. Decisions on anything else (trees, conditions, certificates and the like) are not counted. A figure resting on fewer than 30 decisions is held back. The figures are frozen for this edition and never change.

## How this was made, and what it leaves out

- Every decision each council's own planning register lists, decided between October 2023 and September 2026, that we sorted into one of the 9 kinds of work we track, counted on 2026-10-22 and frozen. Decisions on anything else (trees, conditions, certificates and the like) are not counted, and nor is a decision only a third party holds.
- The councils are those Plan Forecast reads in full and sells reports for: Cheshire West and Chester, Ealing, Leeds, North Yorkshire, Salford, Stockport, Trafford, Wakefield, York. They are not a sample of England, and nothing here is a figure for the country.
- Each application is sorted into a kind of work by its description. "Householder" is the first seven kinds: extensions, lofts and dormers, garden rooms and outbuildings, garage conversions and porches.
- The share approved is of applications granted or refused; a withdrawn application is in neither. Days to decide run from the day an application was validated, or received where no validation day is given, to its decision, over those granted or refused; a decision dated before it was received is a bad date, and not counted in the times. Each time is a day some decision took, so "half within" is true of the decisions counted.
- A figure resting on fewer than 30 decisions is held back (for days to decide, fewer than 30 with the dates to count from), and a council's refusal topics are shown only where we read at least 30 of its refusals and half of them. A refusal whose notice we have not read is counted as a refusal, never as one without reasons.
- These figures differ from the council pages on our site, which are counted afresh each day over the last three years; an edition counts to the end of the month before it and never changes.
- Counts only: no application is named or placed, and nothing a council wrote is quoted.
